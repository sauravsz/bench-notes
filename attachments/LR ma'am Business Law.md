- An independent body.

### Core Latin Legal Maxims
1. ***Ignorantia Juris Non Excusat / Ignorantia Legis Non Excusat***:
   - Ignorance of the law is no excuse.
2. ***Emptor Emit Quam Minimo Potest, Venditor Vendit Quam Maximo Potest***:
   - The buyer buys as cheap as he can; the seller sells as dear/high as he can.
3. ***Contractus Legem Ex Conventione Accipiunt***:
   - Contracts receive/become law from the agreement of their parties.
4. ***Ex Maleficio Non Oritur Contractus***:
   - No contract arises out of a misconduct or illegal act.
5. ***Nemo Debet In Communione Invitus Teneri***:
   - No one should be kept in co-ownership/partnership against their will.
6. ***Nil Temere Novandum***:
   - Nothing should be rashly altered or innovated.
7. ***Universitates Nihil/Tantum Possunt Quod Per Legem Ipsis Datum Est***:
   - Corporations can only do what is permitted by law.
8. ***Aequitas Neminem Laedit Cum Injuria***:
   - Equity injures no one.

### Core Definitions
- **Business**: A system built on promises made between parties and their performance.
- **Breaking of a Promise**: Breaking a promise without incurring liability leads to endless complications in commercial transactions.
- **Law of Contract**: The foundation upon which the superstructure of modern business is built. It lays down legal rules relating to promises, their formation, performance, and enforcement.

## Page 3: Contract, Agreement, Promise & Legal System Factors

### Statutory Definitions (Indian Contract Act, 1872)
- **Section 2(h)**: *"An agreement enforceable by law is a contract."*
- **Section 2(e)**: *"Every promise and every set of promises, forming the consideration for each other, is an agreement."*
- **Section 2(b)**: *"When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise."*

### Juristic Definitions of Contract
- **Pollock**: *"Every agreement and promise enforceable at law is a contract."*
- **Salmond**: *"An agreement creating and defining obligation between parties."*
- **Anson**: *"A legally binding agreement between two or more persons by which rights are acquired by one or more to acts or forbearances on the part of the other."*

### Distinction: Agreement vs. Contract

| Basis | Agreement | Contract |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formula** | Offer + Acceptance | Agreement + Enforceability |
| **Binding Nature** | Not necessarily legally binding | Strictly binding on all concerned parties |
| **Legal Obligation** | May or may not create a legal obligation | Necessarily creates a legal obligation |
| **Scope** | Wider term (All contracts are agreements) | Narrower term (All agreements are NOT contracts) |

### Taxonomy of the Indian Contract Act, 1872
- **General Principles of Law of Contract** (Sections 1–75)
- **Special Contracts**:
  - Indemnity and Guarantee (Sections 124–147)
  - Bailment and Pledge (Sections 148–181)
  - Agency (Sections 182–238)
  - *(Note: Sale of Goods was originally Sections 76–123 and Partnership Sections 239–266, later codified into separate Acts in 1930 & 1932).*

### Factors in the Indian Legal Environment
- **Conducive Factors (Strengths)**:
  - System of Rule of Law.
  - Federal structure promoting legal uniformity.
  - Universal use of English in superior courts.
  - Decisions based on preponderance of probabilities (civil) / beyond reasonable doubt (criminal).
  - Bench system in Supreme Court and High Courts (instead of jury trials).
- **Non-Conducive Factors (Challenges)**:
  - Procedural delays & case backlog.
  - High costs in terms of time and money.
  - Complex legal jargon ("lawyers' paradise").
  - Need for widespread legal aid to expedite justice.

## Page 4: Essentials of a Valid Contract & Key Terminology

### Core Contractual Terms
- **Proposal / Offer (Sec 2(a))**: When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence.
- **Promise (Sec 2(b))**: A proposal when accepted.
- **Promisor & Promisee (Sec 2(c))**: The person making the proposal is the "Promisor"; the person accepting is the "Promisee".
- **Consideration (Sec 2(d))**: When, at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person has done or abstained from doing, or does or abstains from doing, or promises to do or to abstain from doing, something, such act or abstinence or promise is called a consideration for the promise.
- **Agreement (Sec 2(e))**: Every promise and every set of promises forming consideration for each other.
- **Reciprocal Promises (Sec 2(f))**: Promises which form the consideration or part of the consideration for each other.
- **Void Agreement (Sec 2(g))**: An agreement not enforceable by law is void (*Void ab initio* = void from the beginning).
- **Contract (Sec 2(h))**: An agreement enforceable by law.
- **Voidable Contract (Sec 2(i))**: An agreement enforceable by law at the option of one or more of the parties thereto, but not at the option of the other or others.
- **Void Contract (Sec 2(j))**: A contract which ceases to be enforceable by law becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable.

### 10 Essentials of a Valid Contract (Section 10)
1. Proper offer and its proper acceptance.
2. Intention to create legal relationship (not mere social/domestic agreements).
3. Lawful consideration.
4. Lawful object.
5. Capacity of parties to contract (competency).
6. Free consent (Must not be vitiated by Coercion, Undue Influence, Fraud, Misrepresentation, or Mistake).
7. Certainty and definiteness of terms.
8. Possibility of performance (physical and legal).
9. Agreement not expressly declared to be void by law (e.g., in restraint of trade, marriage, or legal proceedings).
10. Legal formalities (compliance with writing, registration, stamp duty where mandated).

## Page 7: Legal Rules as to a Valid Offer

### Mandatory Legal Rules for Offer
1. **Intention to create legal relations**: The offer must be intended to give rise to legal consequences upon acceptance.
2. **Certain, definite, and unambiguous terms**: Vague or indefinite terms prevent contract formation.
3. **Communication**: The offer must be communicated to the offeree (an offer cannot be accepted in ignorance of it).
4. **Made with a view to obtaining assent**: Distinct from casual inquiries or statements.
5. **Cannot contain non-compliance clause**: An offer cannot state that silence or non-communication shall be deemed acceptance.
6. **Distinguished from Invitation to Offer**: Catalogs, price tags, advertisements, tenders, and auction notices are invitations to offer, not offers.
7. **Distinguished from mere Declaration of Intention**: Announcing an auction or intention to sell is not an offer.
8. **Communication of Special Terms**: Specific conditions on receipts/tickets must be reasonably brought to notice.

## Page 8: Acceptance & Rules of Communication

**Date:** 25/08/26 | **Instructor:** LR Ma'am

### Acceptance (Section 2(b))
- **Definition**: *"When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise."*
- Mode: Can be Express or Implied.

### Legal Rules as to Valid Acceptance
1. **Absolute and Unqualified**: Must match the offer exactly ("Mirror Image Rule"). Any conditional acceptance or modification operates as a counter-offer.
2. **Expressed in Usual / Prescribed Manner**: If a specific mode is prescribed, acceptance must follow that mode.
3. **Communicated to Offeror**: Acceptance must be actively communicated; mere mental assent is insufficient.
4. **By Whom**: Must be accepted by the person/group to whom the offer is directed.
5. **To Whom**: Must be communicated to the offeror or authorized agent.
6. **Before Lapse/Revocation of Offer**: Must be given while the offer is alive.

### Communication & Revocation (Sections 3, 4, 5)
- **Communication of Offer (Sec 4)**: Complete when it comes to the knowledge of the person to whom it is made.
- **Communication of Acceptance (Sec 4)**:
  - *As against the proposer*: When put into the course of transmission so as to be out of the power of the acceptor.
  - *As against the acceptor*: When it comes to the knowledge of the proposer.
- **Revocation of Offer & Acceptance (Sec 5)**:
  - *Offer*: May be revoked at any time before communication of its acceptance is complete as against the proposer.
  - *Acceptance*: May be revoked at any time before communication of the acceptance is complete as against the acceptor.

## Page 9: Capacity of Parties, Free Consent & Vitiating Factors

### Capacity to Contract (Section 11)
Every person is competent to contract who:
1. Is of the age of majority according to the law to which they are subject (18 years under the Indian Majority Act, 1875).
2. Is of sound mind.
3. Is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which they are subject.

**Disqualified Persons:**
- *Minors*: Agreements are *void ab initio*.
- *Persons of Unsound Mind*: Idiots, lunatics, drunkards/intoxicated persons.
- *Disqualified by Law*: Alien enemies, foreign sovereigns, convicts, undischarged insolvents.

### Free Consent (Sections 13 & 14)
- **Consent (Sec 13)**: *"Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense"* (*Consensus ad idem*).
- **Free Consent (Sec 14)**: Consent is free when not caused by Coercion (Sec 15), Undue Influence (Sec 16), Fraud (Sec 17), Misrepresentation (Sec 18), or Mistake (Sec 20–22).

### Vitiating Factors in Detail
1. **Coercion (Section 15)**:
   - Committing or threatening to commit any act forbidden by the Indian Penal Code, or unlawful detaining/threatening to detain property, to prejudice any person with the intention of causing any person to enter into an agreement.
   - *Threat to commit suicide* constitutes coercion (*Chikkam Ammiraju v. Seshamma*).
   - *Threat to file a legitimate suit* is not coercion.
   - *Effect (Sec 19)*: Contract is voidable at the option of the aggrieved party.
2. **Undue Influence (Section 16)**:
   - When relations between parties are such that one is in a position to dominate the will of the other and uses that position to obtain an unfair advantage.
   - Arises in real/apparent authority, fiduciary relationships, or mental distress.
   - *Effect (Sec 19A)*: Contract is voidable at the option of the aggrieved party.
3. **Fraud (Section 17)**:
   - Intentional deception to induce agreement:
     1. Suggesting as a fact that which is not true by one who does not believe it to be true.
     2. Active concealment of a fact by one having knowledge or belief of it.
     3. A promise made without any intention of performing it.
     4. Any other act fitted to deceive.
     5. Any act or omission specifically declared fraudulent by law.
   - *Rule of Silence*: Mere silence as to facts is generally not fraud, except where there is a duty to speak (*contracts uberrimae fidei* like insurance, marriage, family settlement, share allotment) or where silence is equivalent to speech.
   - Principle of *Caveat Emptor* (Let the buyer beware).

## Page 10: Classification of Contracts, Misrepresentation, Mistake & Mental Incapacity

**Date:** 27/08/26 | **Instructor:** LR Ma'am

### Classification of Contracts
- **By Enforceability**: Valid, Voidable, Void (*Void ab initio* / Void Contract), Illegal, Unenforceable.
- **By Formation**: Express, Implied, Quasi-Contract, E-Contract.
- **By Extent of Performance**: Executed, Executory (Unilateral, Bilateral).

### Misrepresentation & Mistake
- **Misrepresentation (Section 18)**:
  - Unwarranted positive assertions without deceptive intent, breach of duty without intent to deceive (constructive fraud), or inducing an innocent mistake of fact.
  - *Effect (Sec 19)*: Voidable at the option of the aggrieved party.
- **Mistake (Sections 20, 21, 22)**:
  - *Mistake of Law*:
    - Indian Law: *Ignorantia juris non excusat* (No relief, contract remains valid).
    - Foreign Law: Treated as Mistake of Fact (Void).
  - *Mistake of Fact*:
    - **Bilateral Mistake (Sec 20)**: Both parties under a mistake as to a matter of fact essential to the agreement $\rightarrow$ Agreement is **VOID**.
    - **Unilateral Mistake (Sec 22)**: Only one party under a mistake $\rightarrow$ Contract is **NOT voidable** (Valid), except in cases of mistaken identity of person or nature of transaction.

### Mental Incapacity & Legal Status
- **Idiot**: Devoid of rational thinking from birth. All agreements (except for necessaries of life) are **absolutely void**.
- **Lunatic**: Deranged mental faculties due to disease/strain. Agreements void, except those made during **lucid intervals**.
- **Drunkard / Intoxicated**: Temporarily incapable of rational judgment. Agreements made while intoxicated are void.
- **Disqualified by Law**: Alien enemies, convicts, insolvents, corporations acting *ultra vires*.

## Page 11: Discharge of Contract & Remedies for Breach

### Definition of Discharge
- *"A contract is said to be discharged when it ceases to operate, i.e., when the rights and obligations created by it come to an end."*

### Modes of Discharge of Contract
1. **By Performance (Sections 37–40)**:
   - *Actual Performance*: Parties fulfill their respective obligations.
   - *Attempted Performance / Tender (Sec 38)*: Valid offer of performance refused by the promisee.
2. **By Mutual Agreement / Consent (Sections 62–64)**:
   - *Novation (Sec 62)*: Substituting a new contract for the original one (with same or new parties).
   - *Rescission (Sec 62)*: Mutual cancellation of the contract.
   - *Alteration (Sec 62)*: Changing terms with mutual consent.
   - *Remission / Waiver (Sec 63)*: Dispensing with or remitting part of performance.
   - *Accord and Satisfaction (Sec 63)*: Settlement by accepting an alternative performance.
3. **By Subsequent / Supervening Impossibility (Section 56)** (*Doctrine of Frustration*):
   - Destruction of subject matter, death/incapacity in personal contracts, change of law, war.
4. **By Lapse of Time**: Expiry of limitation period under the Limitation Act (e.g., 3 years for debt recovery).
5. **By Operation of Law**:
   - Death (in personal performance contracts).
   - Insolvency / Bankruptcy.
   - Merger of inferior rights into superior rights.
   - Unauthorized material alteration (renders contract void).
6. **By Breach of Contract (Section 39)**:
   - *Actual Breach*: Failure to perform on due date or during performance.
   - *Anticipatory Breach*: Repudiation before the due date.
7. **By Promisee's Failure to Afford Facilities (Section 67)**.

### Remedies for Breach of Contract
1. **Suit for Rescission (Sections 39, 64, 65)**
2. **Suit for Damages (Sections 73, 74)**:
   - *Ordinary / General Damages* (Naturally arising from breach).
   - *Special Damages* (Arising from special circumstances communicated).
   - *Exemplary / Punitive / Vindictive Damages* (For breach of promise to marry, banker wrongfully dishonoring check).
   - *Nominal Damages* (To recognize legal injury where no actual loss occurred).
   - *Liquidated Damages and Penalty*.
3. **Suit upon Quantum Meruit** (*"As much as earned / in proportion to work done"*).
4. **Suit for Specific Performance** (Under Specific Relief Act, 1963).
5. **Suit for Injunction** (Restraining order against breach of negative covenant).

## Page 12: Specific Relief, Injunctions & Introduction to Companies Act, 2013

### Exceptions: When Specific Performance will NOT be Granted
- Where monetary damages are an adequate remedy.
- Where the contract terms are uncertain or inequitable.
- Where the contract is by its nature revocable.
- Where the contract is made by trustees in breach of their trust.
- Where the contract is of a personal nature (e.g., contract to marry, paint, perform).

### Suit for Injunction
- A court order restraining a person from doing a specific act.
- Mode of securing the specific performance of negative terms/covenants of a contract.

## Page 13: Companies Act, 2013 — Objectives, Structure & Key Shifts

### Major Objectives & Governance Reforms
- Simplify company law and enhance ease of doing business.
- Improve corporate governance, transparency, and accountability.
- Provide stronger regulatory oversight (e.g., NFRA, SFIO, NCLT).
- Introduce **Class Action Suits (Sec 245)** to safeguard minority shareholders.
- Enforce gender diversity: Mandatory appointment of **Women Directors**.
- Ensure board independence: Mandate at least **1/3rd Independent Directors** for listed companies.
- Limit tenure of Independent Directors (Max two consecutive terms of 5 years).
- Make **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)** spending de facto mandatory (Sec 135: 2% of average net profits).
- Improve reporting quality: Mandatory consolidated financial statements covering unlisted subsidiaries and JVs.
- Mandatory statutory auditor rotation (Individual: 5 years; Firm: 10 years).

## Page 14: Comparative Analysis & Business Enterprise Classification

### Key Provisions Compared: 1956 vs. 2013

1. **Applicability**:
   - *1956*: Extended to India with exceptions for J&K / Sikkim.
   - *2013*: Applicable to the entire territory of India (Sec 1).
2. **Maximum Number of Directors**:
   - *1956*: Max 12 directors; central government approval needed to exceed.
   - *2013*: Max 15 directors; can be increased beyond 15 by passing a **Special Resolution (SR)** without government approval (Sec 149(1)).
3. **Women Director**:
   - *1956*: No statutory requirement.
   - *2013*: Mandatory for all listed companies and public companies with paid-up capital $\ge$ ₹100 Cr or turnover $\ge$ ₹300 Cr (Sec 149(1)).
4. **Resident Director**:
   - *1956*: No requirement.
   - *2013*: Every company must have at least one director resident in India for $\ge$ 182 days in the financial year (Sec 149(3)).
5. **E-Governance**:
   - *1956*: Physical documentation and manual filings.
   - *2013*: Mandatory electronic filings, electronic maintenance of records, and e-voting.
6. **Memorandum of Association (MoA) - Object Clause**:
   - *1956*: Split into Main, Ancillary, and Other Objects.
   - *2013*: Bifurcation eliminated; MoA lists Main Objects and matters considered necessary in furtherance thereof.
7. **Articles of Association (AoA) - Entrenchment**:
   - *1956*: No specific entrenchment provisions.
   - *2013*: Specific statutory recognition of entrenchment clauses (Sec 5(3)).
8. **Exit Option for Dissenting Shareholders**:
   - *1956*: No exit mechanism if unutilized IPO funds changed purpose.
   - *2013*: Promoters must provide an exit option to dissenting shareholders if changing objects after raising money from the public (Sec 13(8), 27).
9. **Bonus Shares**:
   - *1956*: Regulated by guidelines and articles.
   - *2013*: Comprehensive statutory rules enacted under Section 63.
10. **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)**:
    - *1956*: Voluntary; no statutory mandate.
    - *2013*: Mandatory CSR Committee and 2% net profit allocation for qualifying companies (Sec 135).

## Page 15: Corporate Jurisprudence & Classification of Companies (Act of 2013)

### Socio-Economic Jurisprudence of a Corporation
- **Traditional / Capitalist View**: A company is private property; shareholders are the absolute owners.
- **Modern Socio-Economic View**: A company is a **social institution**; shareholders are capital suppliers entitled to a reasonable return, while the company owes duties to employees, consumers, suppliers, and society.

### Types of Companies under Companies Act, 2013

#### 1. By Liability
- **Company Limited by Shares (Sec 2(22))**: Liability of members limited by the MoA to the amount unpaid on shares held.
- **Company Limited by Guarantee (Sec 2(21))**: Liability limited to the amount undertaken to be contributed to company assets in the event of winding up.
- **Unlimited Company (Sec 2(92))**: No limit on the liability of its members.

#### 2. By Number of Members & Scale
- **One Person Company (OPC) (Sec 2(62))**: A private company with only 1 person as a member.
- **Private Company (Sec 2(68))**: Restricts share transferability, limits members to 200, and prohibits public subscription.
- **Public Company (Sec 2(71))**: Not a private company; minimum 7 members, no upper limit.
- **Small Company (Sec 2(85))**:
  - Other than a public company.
  - Paid-up share capital does not exceed ₹50 Lakhs (or prescribed up to ₹4 Cr / ₹10 Cr).
  - Turnover does not exceed ₹2 Cr (or prescribed up to ₹40 Cr / ₹100 Cr).
  - *Non-applicability*: Does not apply to holding/subsidiary companies, Section 8 companies, or statutory companies.

#### 3. By Control & Relationship
- **Holding Company (Sec 2(46))**: A company of which other companies are subsidiaries.
- **Subsidiary Company (Sec 2(87))**: A company where the holding company controls board composition or holds $> 50\%$ of voting power.
- **Associate Company (Sec 2(6))**: A company in which another company has *significant influence* ($\ge 20\%$ voting power or control of business decisions), not being a subsidiary, including a joint venture.

#### 4. Special Categories
- **Section 8 Company**: Charitable companies for promoting commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, or environmental protection. Profits are applied to objects; dividend distribution is prohibited.
- **Government Company (Sec 2(45))**: $\ge 51\%$ paid-up share capital held by Central Government, State Government(s), or jointly.
- **Foreign Company (Sec 2(42))**: Incorporated outside India with a place of business in India (physically or electronically) and conducting business activities in India.
- **Listed Company (Sec 2(52))**: Has any of its securities listed on a recognised stock exchange.

## Page 16: Key Statutory Definitions & Incorporation Reforms

### Key Preliminary Definitions
- **Key Managerial Personnel (KMP) (Sec 2(51))**: CEO, Managing Director, Manager, Company Secretary (CS), Whole-time Director, CFO.
- **Independent Director (Sec 149(6))**: Defined with objective criteria of independence and integrity.
- **Auditing Standards (NFRA)**: Statutory recognition and enforcement of mandatory auditing and accounting standards.
- **Promoter (Sec 2(69))**: Statutorily defined based on control and role in prospectus/annual return.
- **Related Party (Sec 2(76)) & Related Party Transactions (Sec 188)**: Comprehensive coverage of directors, KMP, relatives, holding/subsidiary/associate entities.
- **Officer in Default (Sec 2(60))**: Scope expanded to include KMP, share transfer agents, merchant bankers, and registrars.
- **Subsidiary of a Public Company**: Deemed to be a public company for statutory purposes even if its articles retain private company restrictions.
- **Employee Stock Option (ESOP)**: Defined and statutorily permitted for employees/directors of holding and subsidiary companies.

### Reforms in Company Incorporation
- **Simplified MoA Objects Clause**: Elimination of the "other objects" category; focus on main objects and matters incidental thereto.
- **OPC Introduction**: Single entrepreneur incorporation with limited liability.
- **Streamlined Conversion**: Simplified mechanisms for conversion between Private, Public, and OPC structures.
- **Standard Financial Year**: Uniform fiscal year (April 1 to March 31) for all companies (NCLT exceptions for foreign consolidation).
- **RoC Enforcement**: Powers granted to the Registrar of Companies to strike off names of defunct companies or those incorporated through false representations.

## Page 17: Fraudulent Incorporation Penalties & Securities Allotment

**Date:** 18/09/26 | **Instructor:** LR Ma'am

### Fraudulent Incorporation & Penalties
- **Section 447 (Punishment for Fraud)**:
  - Any person deliberately furnishing false or incorrect information or suppressing material facts during incorporation is liable under Section 447 (Non-bailable criminal offense, imprisonment from 6 months up to 10 years and fine).
- **Powers of the Tribunal (Section 7(7))**:
  - Any person can challenge the validity of incorporation before the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).
  - NCLT can order regulation of management, direct changes in MoA/AoA, make liability of members unlimited, or order winding up.

### Provisions on Prospectus & Allotment of Securities (Chapter III)
- **Widened Scope**: Encompasses all **Securities** (shares, debentures, bonds, derivatives) rather than just shares.
- **Methods of Raising Funds by Public Companies**:
  1. Public Offer (Initial Public Offer - IPO / Follow-on Public Offer - FPO)
  2. Private Placement (Section 42)
  3. Rights Issue / Bonus Issue (Sections 62 & 63)
- **Restriction on Fund Utilization**:
  - If a company varies the terms of a contract or objects stated in the prospectus, it is statutorily prohibited from utilizing prospectus funds to buy, trade, or deal in equity shares of any other listed company.
